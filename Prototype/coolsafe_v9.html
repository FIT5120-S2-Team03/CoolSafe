    DO THIS
    Stay Hydrated
    Drink a glass of water every hour — even if you don't feel thirsty. Your body needs fluid to regulate heat.
    DO THIS
    Cool Your Body
    Apply a cool, damp cloth to your neck and wrists. Cooling pulse points lowers your core temperature fast.
    DO THIS
    Find a Cool Space
    Libraries, community centres and shopping centres are free and air-conditioned. Check the map for what's open near you.
    ✕ Avoid this
    AVOID
    Peak Sun Hours
    Avoid being outdoors between 11 AM and 4 PM. Direct sun during peak hours dramatically increases your heat risk.
    AVOID
    Alcohol & Heavy Meals
    Alcohol accelerates dehydration and impairs heat response. Heavy meals generate extra internal heat — both raise your risk.
    AVOID
    Staying Home Alone
    Poorly ventilated homes can exceed outdoor temperatures. Let someone know where you are and check on elderly neighbours.

    Call 000 immediately if you see:
    Heat stroke — hot dry skin, confusion, not sweating
    Severe heat exhaustion — heavy sweating, weakness, pale skin
    Collapse or unconsciousness — any loss of consciousness in heat

    What the temperature means
    Below 28°
    Safe
    Stay hydrated and enjoy outdoor activities at any time of day.
    28–32°
    Caution
    Avoid prolonged sun exposure. Drink frequently and rest during peak hours.
    32–38°
    Heat alert
    Victoria's official threshold. Limit outdoor time. Check on neighbours.

    Extreme
    Stay indoors. Visit a cool space. Call 000 if someone shows heat stroke signs.
